The facts, with sources

The central issue of this site is violence against women (VAW). The Philippine Commission on Women defines it as any act of gender-based violence that results in physical, sexual or psychological harm to women.

17.5%
Of Filipino women aged 15 to 49 experienced physical, sexual or emotional violence from an intimate partner (2022). [2]
1 in 3
Women worldwide, about 736 million, have faced physical and/or sexual violence in their lifetime. [3]
13,213
Violence against women cases reported to the Philippine National Police in 2023. [1]

Rates also differ by region. In 2022, Bicol had the highest share at 26.5%, followed by Ilocos at 25% and MIMAROPA at 23.7%. [4]

Violence has many forms

RA 9262 recognizes that abuse is not only physical. Tap each form to learn more.

Physical violence
Acts that cause bodily harm, such as hitting, slapping, choking or threatening with a weapon. [5]
Sexual violence
Sexual acts committed or forced against a woman or her child, including rape and sexual coercion by a partner. [5]
Psychological violence
Acts or omissions that cause or are likely to cause mental or emotional suffering, such as threats, stalking, humiliation, controlling behavior. [5]
Economic abuse
Controlling a woman's money or preventing her from working, such as withholding financial support or taking her earnings. RA 9262 lists economic abuse as a form of violence. [5]
Online and public harassment
Catcalling, unwanted sexual remarks, stalking and online sexual harassment in streets, workplaces, schools and online fall under the Safe Spaces Act. [7]

Laws that protect women

Know your rights. These Philippine laws address violence and discrimination.

RA 9262 (2004)

Anti-Violence Against Women and Their Children Act. Covers abuse by a husband, ex-husband, live-in or former live-in partner, or a person with whom the woman has or had a dating or sexual relationship. Allows protection orders. [5]

RA 9710 (2009)

Magna Carta of Women. The country's comprehensive women's rights law, aligned with CEDAW. [8]

RA 8353 (1997)

Anti-Rape Law. Reclassified rape as a crime against persons, not just against chastity. [9]

RA 7877 (1995)

Anti-Sexual Harassment Act. Covers work, education and training settings. [10]

RA 11313 (2019)

Safe Spaces Act. Penalizes gender-based sexual harassment in streets, public spaces, online, workplaces and schools. [7]

RA 11596 (2021)

Prohibits child marriage and penalizes those who facilitate it. [11]

What is a protection order?

Under RA 9262, a survivor can ask for a Barangay Protection Order (BPO) from the barangay, or a Temporary or Permanent Protection Order from a court. These orders can direct the abuser to stay away from the survivor. Ask the barangay, a Women and Children Protection Desk, or a lawyer for the current process. [5]

Need help or know someone who does?

If you are in immediate danger, call the national emergency number.

911

You can also reach the Philippine National Police Women and Children Protection Center hotline at 177, or go to the barangay VAW desk or the nearest police Women and Children Protection Desk. Hotline numbers can change, so please confirm them with the Philippine Commission on Women (pcw.gov.ph).
